**Research Question:** To what extent does distribution-aware training (EXP_DIST) enhances out-of-distribution generalization relative to capacity expansion (EXP_CAP), coverage maximization (EXP_COV), and optimization-only baselines (EXP_OPT), and how does this mechanism differ from stochastic regularization (EXP_ACT)?

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** EXP_DIST improves out-of-distribution robustness without proportional gains in in-distribution accuracy, indicating a representational realignment rather than simple performance scaling.

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
1. Capacity Scaling: Observed gains are driven solely by increased effective model capacity/compute (EXP_OPT).
2. Regularization Effect: Improvements stem from implicit noise injection or uncertainty modeling analogous to EXP_ACT.
3. Evaluation Artifact: Differences reflect benchmark-specific sensitivity rather than genuine distributional adaptation.

**Discriminative Experiment:** Execute a fixed-compute ablation on arr06-benchmark-v1 across all six conditions (EXP_CAP, EXP_COV, EXP_DIST, EXP_OPT, EXP_ACT, EXP_PLACEBO). Hold architecture, training steps, and data augmentation pipelines constant. Measure in-distribution accuracy, out-of-distribution success rate, and calibration error. EXP_PLACEBO controls for training-time stochasticity.

**Predicted Outcomes:** EXP_DIST will decouple OOD robustness from in-distribution accuracy, maintaining high OOD performance with flat ID curves. EXP_CAP and EXP_COV will show correlated ID/OOD improvements. EXP_OPT and EXP_ACT will track EXP_PLACEBO on OOD metrics despite ID gains.

**Falsification Condition:** The hypothesis is rejected if EXP_DIST shows no statistically significant OOD advantage over EXP_PLACEBO when matched for in-distribution performance, or if EXP_OPT/EXP_ACT replicate the EXP_DIST OOD profile under identical compute constraints.
