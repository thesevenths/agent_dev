**Research Question:** Does coverage-focused training (EXP_COV) yield superior zero-shot generalization under distribution shift compared to capacity-matched standard optimization (EXP_OPT) and placebo baselines (EXP_PLACEBO)?

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** Explicitly optimizing for environmental coverage during policy training improves zero-shot transfer performance on unseen distributions beyond what is achieved by capacity-matched standard optimization or placebo training, independent of parameter count and compute budget.

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
1. Observed gains stem from implicit regularization due to diverse sampling, not the explicit objective.
2. Performance differences disappear when model capacity and training steps are strictly equated across variants.
3. Generalization is dictated by input-data density rather than the training objective formulation.

**Discriminative Experiment:** Train EXP_COV, EXP_OPT, EXP_CAP, and EXP_PLACEBO on arr06-benchmark-v1 with identical architectures, optimizers, and total step counts. Evaluate zero-shot performance on three held-out distribution shifts using standardized metrics (success rate, collision frequency, L2 trajectory error). Include EXP_DIST and EXP_ACT as secondary controls to isolate coverage-specific effects.

**Predicted Outcomes:** EXP_COV will outperform EXP_OPT and EXP_PLACEBO by ≥5% in success rate under distribution shift, with no significant difference between EXP_OPT and EXP_PLACEBO. EXP_CAP will match EXP_OPT but underperform EXP_COV. EXP_DIST and EXP_ACT will show intermediate, objective-consistent performance.

**Falsification Condition:** The hypothesis is falsified if EXP_COV fails to exceed EXP_OPT and EXP_PLACEBO by the pre-registered threshold under controlled capacity/compute conditions, or if performance rankings reverse when training duration or data diversity is systematically varied.
