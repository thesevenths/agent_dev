**Research Question:** How do optimization trajectories and distributional shifts interact with model capacity to determine generalization performance within the arr06-benchmark-v1 training regime?

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** Under distributional shift, high-capacity models will exhibit significantly higher generalization error than low-capacity models unless optimization is constrained to smooth loss landscapes; this interaction will remain significant after controlling for input-space coverage and activation statistics.

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
1. Generalization is primarily driven by input-space coverage (EXP_COV), making capacity and optimization path secondary predictors.
2. Performance is determined by activation dynamics (EXP_ACT), with distributional shift and capacity showing negligible main effects.
3. Observed performance variations are attributable to baseline noise or placebo effects (EXP_PLACEBO) rather than systematic interactions among capacity, shift, and optimization.

**Discriminative Experiment:** A 2×3×2 factorial design manipulating capacity (low/high), distributional shift (none/moderate/severe), and optimization schedule (standard/unconstrained vs. landscape-smoothed/early-stopped), evaluated on covariate-shifted test splits. EXP_PLACEBO runs provide null baselines, while EXP_COV and EXP_ACT metrics are logged as covariates for statistical control.

**Predicted Outcomes:** The highest generalization error will occur in the high-capacity + severe shift + unconstrained optimization condition. Landscape-smoothed optimization will significantly reduce error across capacities. Coverage and activation metrics will show weak partial correlations with error once capacity and shift are accounted for.

**Falsification Condition:** The hypothesis is falsified if (i) high-capacity models consistently outperform low-capacity models under severe shift despite unconstrained optimization, (ii) optimization schedule shows no statistically significant interaction with capacity and shift, or (iii) coverage/activation metrics fully mediate the relationship between capacity/shift and generalization error.
