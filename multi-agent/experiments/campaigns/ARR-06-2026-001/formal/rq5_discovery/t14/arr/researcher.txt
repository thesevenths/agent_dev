**Research Question:** How do training objectives targeting distributional robustness (EXP_DIST), capacity scaling (EXP_CAP), and optimization speed (EXP_OPT) compare to a placebo baseline (EXP_PLACEBO) in driving out-of-distribution generalization and sample efficiency on the arr06-benchmark-v1?

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** Distribution-aware training (EXP_DIST) produces statistically significant gains in out-of-distribution success rates relative to both capacity-focused (EXP_CAP) and optimization-focused (EXP_OPT) training, while maintaining equivalent or superior sample efficiency.

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
1. Capacity scaling (EXP_CAP) improves generalization by expanding effective state coverage, outperforming distribution-aware methods.
2. Optimization speed (EXP_OPT) is the primary driver of final performance, rendering distribution-specific objectives redundant.
3. All non-placebo variants perform indistinguishably from the placebo baseline under standard evaluation metrics.

**Discriminative Experiment:** Run all six experimental conditions (EXP_CAP, EXP_COV, EXP_DIST, EXP_OPT, EXP_ACT, EXP_PLACEBO) on arr06-benchmark-v1 using identical network architecture, hyperparameters, random seeds, and data pipelines. Evaluate each on held-out distribution-shifted tasks while logging cumulative reward, out-of-distribution success rate, and samples-to-90% asymptotic performance.

**Predicted Outcomes:** EXP_DIST achieves the highest OOD success rate with moderate sample efficiency; EXP_OPT converges fastest but plateaus at lower OOD performance; EXP_CAP shows intermediate OOD results with higher variance; EXP_PLACEBO remains at baseline/chance levels; EXP_COV and EXP_ACT fall between EXP_DIST and EXP_PLACEBO.

**Falsification Condition:** The hypothesis is falsified if (a) no statistically significant difference (p > 0.05) exists between EXP_DIST and EXP_PLACEBO in OOD success rates, or (b) EXP_OPT matches or exceeds EXP_DIST in both convergence speed and OOD performance across all evaluated tasks.
