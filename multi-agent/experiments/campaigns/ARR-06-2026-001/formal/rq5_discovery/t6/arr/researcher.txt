**Research Question:** To what extent does integrating capacity-constrained training (EXP_CAP) with distributional perturbation (EXP_DIST) improve convergence stability over placebo-controlled baselines (EXP_PLACEBO), and can this effect be mechanistically separated from increased coverage (EXP_COV)?

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** EXP_CAP + EXP_DIST reduces convergence variance by ≥20% relative to EXP_PLACEBO without proportional increases in coverage entropy, indicating a regularization mechanism rather than a sampling artifact.

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
- H₀ (Null): EXP_CAP + EXP_DIST performs equivalently to EXP_PLACEBO when matched for compute budget and initialization.
- H₁ (Coverage-mediated): Stability gains are fully explained by expanded coverage (EXP_COV), such that variance reduction correlates r > 0.8 with coverage metrics.

**Discriminative Experiment:** A controlled factorial evaluation across EXP_CAP, EXP_DIST, EXP_COV, and EXP_PLACEBO on arr06-benchmark-v1, holding total training steps, architecture, and seed distributions constant; primary metric is convergence variance, secondary metric is coverage entropy.

**Predicted Outcomes:** Under the primary hypothesis, EXP_CAP + EXP_DIST yields low variance with neutral coverage entropy; under H₁, variance decreases only when coverage entropy increases, and statistical control for coverage eliminates the treatment effect.

**Falsification Condition:** The hypothesis is falsified if (i) EXP_CAP + EXP_DIST shows no statistically significant variance reduction versus EXP_PLACEBO (p ≥ 0.05, Bonferroni-corrected), or (ii) the effect size vanishes after covariate adjustment for coverage metrics, confirming H₁.
