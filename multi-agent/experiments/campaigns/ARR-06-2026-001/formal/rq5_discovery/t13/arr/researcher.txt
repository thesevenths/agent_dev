**Research Question:** In the arr06-benchmark-v1 training family, how do distinct objective formulations (EXP_CAP, EXP_COV, EXP_DIST, EXP_OPT, EXP_ACT, and EXP_PLACEBO) differentially impact agent convergence speed, policy stability, and task success?

**Primary Hypothesis:** Distribution-aware training (EXP_DIST) will yield significantly faster convergence and higher task success rates than placebo (EXP_PLACEBO) and covariance-matched (EXP_COV) baselines, while maintaining lower trajectory variance than optimization-only (EXP_OPT) and action-penalized (EXP_ACT) approaches.

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
- H₁: Direct optimization (EXP_OPT) dominates all other formulations by maximizing immediate reward signals.
- H₂: Regularization via covariance (EXP_COV) or capacity constraints (EXP_CAP) improves stability but degrades peak performance relative to unstructured training.
- H₃: All structured objectives perform statistically indistinguishably from placebo when initialization, learning rates, and compute budgets are controlled.

**Discriminative Experiment:** Conduct a controlled cross-validation across EXP_DIST, EXP_OPT, EXP_COV, and EXP_PLACEBO on identical arr06-benchmark-v1 tracks, holding network architecture, batch size, and random seeds constant; measure convergence steps, success rate, and policy entropy over 50 independent trials per condition.

**Predicted Outcomes:** EXP_DIST will show the lowest median convergence steps and highest success rate with moderate entropy; EXP_OPT will exhibit high success but elevated variance and entropy; EXP_COV/CAP will stabilize early but plateau below EXP_DIST; EXP_PLACEBO will establish the baseline performance floor.

**Falsification Condition:** The primary hypothesis is falsified if EXP_DIST fails to significantly outperform EXP_PLACEBO in convergence speed or success rate (p > 0.05), or if EXP_OPT achieves equal or superior performance across all metrics without increased variance.
