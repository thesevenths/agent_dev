**Research Question:** To what extent do capacity constraints and distributional shifts interact to determine policy convergence stability during training, relative to activation scaling and state-space coverage?

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** Policy convergence rate degrades predictably as capacity constraints tighten and distributional shift increases, with the CAP×DIST interaction term accounting for ≥40% of variance in training instability, independent of activation scaling or coverage metrics.

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
1. Coverage bias (EXP_COV) is the primary driver of instability, not capacity or distribution.
2. Activation scaling (EXP_ACT) fully mediates the CAP×DIST effect, neutralizing degradation when properly tuned.
3. Optimizer dynamics (EXP_OPT) dominate convergence behavior, rendering CAP, DIST, ACT, and COV secondary.

**Discriminative Experiment:** A full factorial design across EXP_CAP (low/high), EXP_DIST (nominal/moderate/severe), EXP_ACT (baseline/adaptive/rescaled), and EXP_COV (sparse/dense), evaluated on arr06-benchmark-v1. EXP_PLACEBO provides the unconstrained baseline. EXP_OPT is fixed to a standard adaptive optimizer to isolate structural factors. Convergence rate and loss variance are tracked per epoch.

**Predicted Outcomes:** Under the main hypothesis, high CAP + severe DIST yields significantly lower convergence rates than any single-factor condition, with negligible ACT or COV main effects. Competitor 1 predicts COV-dominant variance; Competitor 2 predicts ACT×DIST interaction nullifies CAP effects; Competitor 3 predicts OPT configuration overrides all other factors.

**Falsification Condition:** The hypothesis is falsified if regression analysis shows the CAP×DIST interaction explains <20% of variance in convergence rate, or if ACT or COV consistently demonstrates stronger main effects than CAP/DIST across all benchmark tasks.
