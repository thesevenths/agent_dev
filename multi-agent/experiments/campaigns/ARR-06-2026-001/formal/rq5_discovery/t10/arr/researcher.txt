**Research Question:** To what extent does objective optimization during training improve out-of-distribution generalization relative to capacity-constrained, coverage-focused, distribution-shifted, action-clipped, and placebo-controlled training protocols on the arr06-benchmark-v1?

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** Training with EXP_OPT yields statistically significant improvements in task success and robustness metrics compared to EXP_CAP, EXP_COV, EXP_DIST, EXP_ACT, and EXP_PLACEBO under distribution-shifted evaluation conditions.

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
- Distributional Exposure Dominance: EXP_DIST will outperform all other conditions due to explicit environmental variability during training.
- Capacity Regularization: EXP_CAP will match or exceed EXP_OPT performance because parameter constraints inherently limit overfitting to training artifacts.
- Null Effect: EXP_PLACEBO will perform equivalently to active conditions, indicating no systematic training protocol advantage.

**Discriminative Experiment:** Conduct a controlled cross-validation study across all six experimental conditions (EXP_CAP, EXP_COV, EXP_DIST, EXP_OPT, EXP_ACT, EXP_PLACEBO) using identical model architectures, random seeds, and data splits on arr06-benchmark-v1, with performance measured via standardized success rate and error variance across held-out distribution shifts.

**Predicted Outcomes:** EXP_OPT will achieve the highest mean success rate and lowest performance variance; EXP_PLACEBO will rank lowest. EXP_DIST and EXP_ACT will occupy intermediate positions depending on distributional robustness versus action-space sensitivity, while EXP_CAP and EXP_COV will fall between OPT and PLACEBO based on regularization strength.

**Falsification Condition:** The hypothesis is falsified if any alternative condition (EXP_CAP, EXP_COV, EXP_DIST, EXP_ACT) demonstrates non-inferior or superior performance to EXP_OPT (p > 0.05 after multiple-comparison correction), or if EXP_PLACEBO exceeds EXP_OPT performance.
