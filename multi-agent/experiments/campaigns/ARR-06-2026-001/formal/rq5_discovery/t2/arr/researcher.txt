**Research Question:** To what extent do distinct training interventions—capacity regularization (EXP_CAP), covariance matching (EXP_COV), distribution alignment (EXP_DIST), optimization trajectory shaping (EXP_OPT), activation scaling (EXP_ACT), and placebo controls (EXP_PLACEBO)—differentially impact out-of-distribution generalization on the arr06-benchmark-v1?

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** Distribution alignment (EXP_DIST) and covariance matching (EXP_COV) will produce statistically significant improvements in OOD generalization metrics compared to capacity-constrained (EXP_CAP) or optimization-focused (EXP_OPT) variants, while placebo conditions will not exceed standard training baselines.

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
1. Generalization gains are primarily driven by optimization stability (EXP_OPT) rather than distributional or covariance adjustments.
2. Capacity constraints (EXP_CAP) are the dominant factor, with distribution/covariance interventions yielding negligible additional benefit.
3. All active interventions perform equivalently to the placebo due to uncontrolled hyperparameter confounds.

**Discriminative Experiment:** Independent training runs of each experiment variant on arr06-benchmark-v1 using identical architectures, data splits, and random seeds, followed by evaluation on held-out distribution-shifted test sets. Performance will be compared via one-way ANOVA with Tukey HSD post-hoc tests to isolate intervention-specific effects.

**Predicted Outcomes:** EXP_DIST and EXP_COV will demonstrate >5% relative improvement in OOD metrics over baseline; EXP_OPT and EXP_CAP will show marginal gains (<2%); EXP_ACT and EXP_PLACEBO will remain within baseline confidence intervals.

**Falsification Condition:** The hypothesis is falsified if no active experiment significantly outperforms the placebo (p > 0.05) or if all variants converge to statistically indistinguishable OOD performance distributions, indicating that the tested training mechanisms do not differentially govern generalization behavior.
