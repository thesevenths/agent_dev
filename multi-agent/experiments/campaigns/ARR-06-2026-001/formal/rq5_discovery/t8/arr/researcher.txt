**Research Question:** How do isolated manipulations of capacity, coverage, distribution, optimization, activation, and placebo conditions during the training phase differentially impact model convergence and generalization on the arr06-benchmark-v1?

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** Capacity and distribution perturbations will induce non-linear performance degradation beyond defined thresholds, whereas optimization and activation adjustments will yield monotonic improvements that saturate at intermediate values, with placebo conditions remaining statistically equivalent to baseline.

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
1. *Synergistic Interaction:* Performance variance is driven primarily by cross-factor interactions rather than independent main effects.
2. *Baseline Dominance:* Unmodified (placebo/baseline) configurations outperform targeted experimental manipulations due to reduced sensitivity to perturbed training signals.
3. *Distribution Primacy:* Data distribution shift alone accounts for the majority of performance variance, rendering capacity, coverage, optimization, and activation manipulations statistically negligible.

**Discriminative Experiment:** A fully crossed factorial design isolating each experimental condition (EXP_CAP, EXP_COV, EXP_DIST, EXP_OPT, EXP_ACT, EXP_PLACEBO) using orthogonal parameter sweeps during training, evaluated via standardized convergence trajectories and hold-out validation metrics, with strict control for initialization seeds and computational resources.

**Predicted Outcomes:** EXP_DIST and EXP_CAP will exhibit threshold-dependent performance drops; EXP_OPT and EXP_ACT will show monotonic gains plateauing at mid-range settings; EXP_COV will display moderate linear scaling; EXP_PLACEBO will match baseline variance. Interaction terms between factors will be non-significant.

**Falsification Condition:** The hypothesis is falsified if (i) placebo conditions significantly exceed active manipulations, (ii) any single factor explains >80% of outcome variance without meaningful interactions, or (iii) the predicted non-linear versus linear response curves are empirically reversed or absent across experimental arms.
