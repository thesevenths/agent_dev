**Research Question:** How do model capacity, optimization strategy, and activation function independently and jointly influence distribution-shift robustness during training on the arr06-benchmark-v1?

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** Scaling model capacity without corresponding adjustments to the optimization schedule will not yield statistically significant improvements in out-of-distribution generalization compared to the placebo baseline.

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
- H₁ (Synergistic Scaling): Robustness gains require joint increases in capacity and adaptive optimization.
- H₂ (Non-Linearity Dominance): Activation function choice drives OOD performance, making capacity and optimizer variations marginal.
- H₃ (Null Effect): All experimental configurations perform within the variance bounds of the placebo control.

**Discriminative Experiment:** A controlled factorial study on arr06-benchmark-v1 isolating EXP_CAP (parameter count scaling), EXP_OPT (fixed-step vs. adaptive schedulers), and EXP_ACT (standard vs. alternative non-linearities), with EXP_DIST applied via covariate perturbation aligned with EXP_COV protocols. EXP_PLACEBO serves as the unmodified baseline. All runs share identical data splits, regularization, and evaluation metrics.

**Predicted Outcomes:** Under H₁, large-capacity + adaptive optimizer pairs exceed placebo by ≥5% on OOD metrics. Under H₂, activation swaps produce equivalent gains regardless of capacity/optimizer settings. Under H₃, no arm diverges significantly from placebo.

**Falsification Condition:** The primary hypothesis is falsified if any capacity-increased model paired with a fixed-step optimizer achieves OOD performance overlapping the placebo baseline’s 95% confidence interval, with no statistically significant main or interaction effects detected (p > 0.05, two-way ANOVA).
