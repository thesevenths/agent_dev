**Research Question:** How does optimizing for state-space coverage during training affect out-of-distribution robustness of locomotion policies relative to standard reward-maximization objectives, when controlling for model capacity and activation regularization?

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** Policies trained with explicit coverage objectives (EXP_COV) will achieve significantly higher zero-shot transfer returns under shifted dynamics distributions (EXP_DIST) than policies trained with standard optimization objectives (EXP_OPT), independent of network capacity (EXP_CAP) and activation regularization (EXP_ACT).

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
1. Capacity-driven hypothesis: Transfer performance is determined exclusively by model capacity (EXP_CAP), with no independent contribution from the training objective.
2. Regularization-interaction hypothesis: The performance advantage of coverage-based training is nullified when activation regularization (EXP_ACT) is applied, indicating that observed benefits are confounded with implicit regularization rather than true distributional robustness.

**Discriminative Experiment:** A full factorial design across EXP_CAP (low/high), EXP_OPT vs EXP_COV, and EXP_ACT (off/on) using arr06-benchmark-v1. Agents are evaluated on zero-shot transfer tasks with systematically perturbed physical parameters (EXP_DIST). Performance is quantified as normalized return relative to in-distribution training metrics. EXP_PLACEBO controls for architectural bias and random initialization variance.

**Predicted Outcomes:**
- Main hypothesis: EXP_COV consistently outperforms EXP_OPT in shifted distributions across all CAP/ACT configurations, with minimal interaction terms.
- Capacity-driven: High-CAP models dominate low-CAP models regardless of objective; EXP_COV vs EXP_OPT differences are statistically indistinguishable.
- Regularization-interaction: EXP_COV advantage collapses or reverses when EXP_ACT is enabled, showing strong objective × activation interaction.

**Falsification Condition:** The main hypothesis is falsified if EXP_COV models do not statistically outperform EXP_OPT models in out-of-distribution evaluations when capacity and activation settings are matched, or if the performance differential falls within the confidence bounds of the EXP_PLACEBO control.
