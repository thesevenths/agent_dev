**Research Question:** Under distributional shift (EXP_DIST), does increasing model capacity (EXP_CAP) improve training sample efficiency relative to covariance-aware regularization (EXP_COV) when optimization (EXP_OPT) and activation (EXP_ACT) are held constant, compared to a placebo baseline (EXP_PLACEBO) on arr06-benchmark-v1?

**Primary Hypothesis:** Capacity expansion yields greater sample efficiency gains than covariance-aware regularization under distributional shift.

**Competing Hypotheses:** Covariance-aware regularization provides superior sample efficiency; neither intervention improves over the placebo baseline; performance differences are statistically indistinguishable.

**Discriminative Experiment:** Conduct parallel training runs on arr06-benchmark-v1 using EXP_CAP, EXP_COV, and EXP_PLACEBO configurations, applying identical distributional shift protocols (EXP_DIST) while fixing EXP_OPT and EXP_ACT hyperparameters. Measure episodes-to-convergence and validation loss trajectories across repeated seeds.

**Predicted Outcomes:** If the primary hypothesis holds, EXP_CAP will achieve target performance thresholds in fewer episodes than EXP_COV and EXP_PLACEBO. If the first competing hypothesis holds, EXP_COV will converge fastest. If the second/third hold, all three conditions will show equivalent convergence metrics and loss curves.

**Falsification Condition:** The primary hypothesis is falsified if EXP_COV or EXP_PLACEBO demonstrates statistically significant improvement in convergence speed or final performance over EXP_CAP (α = 0.05, Bonferroni-corrected), or if confidence intervals for all three conditions overlap substantially.
