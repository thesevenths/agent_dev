**Research Question:** How do joint capacity constraints, coverage breadth, and distributional alignment interact to determine training convergence and final performance relative to optimization objective design and action selection dynamics?

**Hypothesis:** Final training performance is primarily governed by the simultaneous satisfaction of capacity limits (EXP_CAP), coverage (EXP_COV), and distributional alignment (EXP_DIST), with marginal dependence on the specific optimization objective formulation.

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
- H1: Optimization objective quality (EXP_OPT) is the dominant driver of performance; structural constraints (EXP_CAP, EXP_COV, EXP_DIST) produce negligible variance.
- H2: Action selection behavior (EXP_ACT) dictates convergence trajectories, rendering capacity/coverage/distribution factors secondary.

**Discriminative Experiment:** Conduct a factorial ablation on arr06-benchmark-v1 where EXP_CAP, EXP_COV, and EXP_DIST are systematically varied across all combinations while holding EXP_OPT and EXP_ACT constant. Include EXP_PLACEBO as a control baseline. Track convergence rate, loss stability, and final task metrics across runs.

**Predicted Outcomes:** 
- Primary hypothesis supported: High-performing configurations cluster exclusively in the EXP_CAP × EXP_COV × EXP_DIST high-satisfaction region, regardless of OPT variant.
- H1 supported: Performance rankings align strictly with EXP_OPT configurations; CAP/COV/DIST variations yield flat performance curves.
- H2 supported: EXP_ACT manipulations explain the majority of performance variance; CAP/COV/DIST changes show no statistically meaningful impact.

**Falsification Condition:** The primary hypothesis is falsified if multivariate analysis reveals no significant three-way interaction among EXP_CAP, EXP_COV, and EXP_DIST on performance metrics, while EXP_OPT or EXP_ACT configurations independently account for ≥70% of explained variance.
