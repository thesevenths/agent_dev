**Research Question:** To what extent do capacity, covariance regularization, distribution alignment, optimization dynamics, activation design, and placebo-controlled noise independently and jointly determine out-of-distribution robustness in the arr06-benchmark-v1 training regime?

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** Under distribution shift, performance degradation is primarily determined by optimization dynamics and covariance regularization, whereas model capacity and activation choice contribute negligibly to robustness once baseline training stability is achieved.

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
1. Capacity Hypothesis: Scaling model capacity linearly improves OOD generalization regardless of optimization or regularization settings.
2. Activation Hypothesis: Nonlinear activation properties dictate gradient flow and are the dominant factor in distributional robustness.
3. Noise-as-Regularization Hypothesis: Stochastic perturbations in the placebo condition produce regularization effects indistinguishable from structured covariance control.

**Discriminative Experiment:** A controlled factorial ablation across EXP_CAP, EXP_COV, EXP_OPT, and EXP_ACT on arr06-benchmark-v1, with EXP_DIST fixed at a moderate shift level and EXP_PLACEBO run as a matched-noise control. Each configuration is evaluated on in-distribution accuracy, OOD accuracy, and convergence stability metrics across three random seeds.

**Predicted Outcomes:** If the primary hypothesis holds, EXP_OPT and EXP_COV will show statistically significant main effects on OOD accuracy and convergence variance, while EXP_CAP and EXP_ACT will yield performance within confidence intervals of the baseline. The EXP_PLACEBO condition will perform equivalently to unstructured noise controls, indicating no latent regularization benefit.

**Falsification Condition:** The hypothesis is falsified if EXP_CAP or EXP_ACT produce statistically significant improvements in OOD accuracy when EXP_OPT and EXP_COV are held at baseline levels, or if EXP_PLACEBO significantly outperforms matched unstructured noise controls under identical computational budgets.
