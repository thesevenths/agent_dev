**Research Question:** To what extent does optimizing action-space constraints (EXP_OPT) improve training convergence and asymptotic performance compared to expanding state coverage (EXP_COV), modulating reward distributions (EXP_DIST), and imposing capacity limits (EXP_CAP), relative to placebo-controlled baselines?

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** Agents trained under EXP_OPT will demonstrate significantly faster convergence and higher final-task performance than those under EXP_COV, EXP_DIST, or EXP_CAP, with no statistically meaningful difference from EXP_ACT.

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
1. Distributional Adaptation: EXP_DIST yields superior robustness and final performance by exposing agents to broader outcome variances.
2. Coverage-Driven Generalization: EXP_COV produces better out-of-distribution transfer, rendering constraint optimization ineffective.
3. Null/Placebo Equivalence: All active interventions perform identically to EXP_PLACEBO, indicating no causal impact of the manipulated variables.

**Discriminative Experiment:** Independent training runs on arr06-benchmark-v1 isolating each intervention (EXP_CAP, EXP_COV, EXP_DIST, EXP_OPT, EXP_ACT, EXP_PLACEBO) under identical hyperparameters and random seeds. Performance is evaluated via epoch-wise reward trajectories, convergence thresholds, and zero-shot transfer accuracy on held-out scenarios.

**Predicted Outcomes:** EXP_OPT exhibits the steepest early-phase learning slope and highest performance plateau; EXP_COV and EXP_DIST show delayed but comparable plateaus; EXP_CAP and EXP_PLACEBO remain at baseline performance levels.

**Falsification Condition:** The hypothesis is falsified if EXP_COV or EXP_DIST achieves equal or superior convergence speed and asymptotic performance to EXP_OPT (α = 0.05, Cohen’s d ≤ 0.2), or if all active conditions fail to diverge significantly from EXP_PLACEBO.
