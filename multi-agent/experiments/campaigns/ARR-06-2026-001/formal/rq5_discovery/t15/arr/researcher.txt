**Research Question:** To what extent do covariance regularization and distribution-aware sampling during training improve sample efficiency and robustness on arr06-benchmark-v1 compared to capacity-constrained, standard optimization, and activation-scaled baselines?

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** Training policies with explicit covariance regularization (EXP_COV) and distribution-aware sampling (EXP_DIST) will produce significantly lower variance in episode returns and faster early-stage convergence than capacity-constrained (EXP_CAP) or unregularized optimization (EXP_OPT) approaches, while achieving equivalent asymptotic performance.

**Competing Hypotheses:** 
1. Performance gains are driven primarily by capacity constraints (EXP_CAP), which limit representational overfitting regardless of covariance or distribution modeling.
2. Increased activation/action complexity (EXP_ACT) improves gradient stability and outperforms both covariance and distribution methods.
3. All experimental variants converge to statistically indistinguishable performance when compute and data budgets are matched, indicating no structural training advantage.

**Discriminative Experiment:** Execute EXP_COV, EXP_DIST, EXP_CAP, EXP_OPT, and EXP_ACT on arr06-benchmark-v1 under identical initialization, random seeds, hardware, and total training steps; include EXP_PLACEBO as a null baseline tracking raw reward noise. Record per-episode returns, convergence epochs, and out-of-distribution generalization metrics across three independent runs.

**Predicted Outcomes:** EXP_COV and EXP_DIST will exhibit 15–25% lower return variance and reach 80% of asymptotic performance ~20% faster than EXP_CAP and EXP_OPT. EXP_ACT will track closely with EXP_OPT. EXP_PLACEBO will show baseline stochasticity with no systematic improvement. Final asymptotic scores will not differ significantly across groups.

**Falsification Condition:** The hypothesis is falsified if EXP_COV and EXP_DIST fail to demonstrate statistically significant reductions in return variance (p < 0.05, two-tailed, Bonferroni-corrected) relative to EXP_CAP/EXP_OPT across all runs, or if their convergence curves fall below baseline trajectories after epoch 50, or if asymptotic performance differs significantly between groups.
